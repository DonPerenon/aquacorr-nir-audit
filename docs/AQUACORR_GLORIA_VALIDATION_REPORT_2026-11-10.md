## Question and status

The prior [AquaCorr native-NIR audit release](https://github.com/DonPerenon/aquacorr-nir-audit/tree/v0.1.1) counted unexpectedly high `passed_qc` NIR values but could not decide whether they reflected water or scene/processing problems. This follow-up tests a selected subset against original field spectra and samples quality masks for the same satellite acquisitions. It narrows that specific evidence gap; it does **not** validate a universal NIR cutoff, establish a representative pixel-error rate, identify one causal processing fault, or prove journal publishability.

The original [AquaCorr-Global v1 record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21927415) already publishes a GLORIA radiometric-validation subset and claims close agreement in visible bands. Therefore this is an **instrument-independent, NIR-specific reanalysis of overlapping source material**, not a new untouched external holdout. The original [GLORIA record](https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.948492) contains spectra over 350–900 nm; it cannot test SWIR. The AquaCorr publisher explicitly cautions about shoreline pixels in narrow channels. Our tests neither repeat nor refute its visible-band assessment.

## Protocol and data provenance

The field comparison was locally fixed in [`protocol.json`](../data/aquacorr_validation/protocol.json) before field-versus-satellite outcomes were first calculated. The global high-NIR audit and a feasibility count of GLORIA records and high values were already known. The ARP run was initiated after the field and pilot scene findings were known; its local timestamp is **not** external preregistration. The separate scene pilot selection rules were saved in [`scene_pilot_protocol.json`](../data/aquacorr_validation/scene_pilot_protocol.json) before selecting pilot records or reading their QA, but after seeing the earlier GLORIA scene findings. All reviewer-triggered diagnostics below are post hoc.

The AquaCorr compressed CSV matched its publisher MD5 and has local SHA-256 `ea44b8632ee57b6b214410282fa0ab5b6cc16b5b32477f842349552f1d82877c`. The original GLORIA ZIP has SHA-256 `f5ad5106d5fd74f0d874085a23e4189302e7409aed9f82d629ae6cdb47d21f14`. All downloaded [Copernicus Sentinel-2](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s2-documents) and [EUMETSAT Landsat 7](https://nwp-saf.eumetsat.int/downloads/rtcoef_info/visir_srf/rtcoef_landsat_7_tm_srf.html) / [Landsat 8](https://nwp-saf.eumetsat.int/downloads/rtcoef_info/visir_srf/rtcoef_landsat_8_oli_srf.html) response files are hashed in [`gloria_nir_registry.json`](../data/aquacorr_validation/gloria_nir_registry.json). Raw source archives are not derivative results and are not redistributed here.

The [source-to-pair code](../data/aquacorr_validation/validate_gloria.py) joins exact `source=GLORIA` AquaCorr rows to original PANGAEA `GLORIA_ID` metadata, quality flags, and one-nanometre `Rrs` spectra. The primary analysis requires `passed_qc=True`, finite sensor-native satellite NIR, original `Flagged=0`, the same UTC calendar date, at most three hours between observations, and at most 100 m between archive and field coordinates. The response-coverage rule is the fraction

`integral_350^900 RSR(lambda) d(lambda) / integral_all RSR(lambda) d(lambda) >= 0.99`.

It is the **band response area inside the GLORIA wavelength interval**, not the fraction of the broad GLORIA range covered by a narrow NIR band. The field spectrum must also be finite at response-grid points with non-negligible weight. Field-band NIR is the response-weighted integral of the original field spectrum on its 1-nm grid. The response-weighted band is Landsat 7 ETM+ band 4, Landsat 8 OLI band 5, or Sentinel-2A/B B8A. Landsat 5 has no response curve in this analysis and is excluded from the primary pair comparison. No single-wavelength surrogate was substituted. Copernicus Sentinel-2 response v5 versus v4 was checked separately.

The preplanned ARP statistic first averages signed satellite-minus-field NIR `Rrs` differences within each country-plus-waterbody label, then averages those waterbody means equally. Its 1,999-resample waterbody bootstrap is descriptive and conditional on the selected labels, not confirmatory inference. Pair and cluster data are in [`primary_pairs_for_arp.csv`](../data/aquacorr_validation/primary_pairs_for_arp.csv), and the computed `R001` registry is retained in the ARP run. Same-input reproduction matched exactly.

## Field comparison results

The **first-exclusion-reason** flow is shown below. Reasons are assigned sequentially by the actual code; a row can have more than one problem. Thus these counts must not be interpreted as independent categories. `R001` reports zero exclusions *after* creation of the 348-row analysis file; it says nothing about the 1,187 upstream exclusions.

| First screen outcome | Matchups | Satellite NIR >0.30 sr⁻¹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Landsat 5 without secured response curve | 119 | 2 |
| Satellite quality flag failed | 364 | 5 |
| Field NIR unavailable | 571 | 34 |
| More than three hours apart | 133 | 3 |
| Eligible | 348 | 8 |
| **All `source=GLORIA` matchups** | **1,535** | **52** |

A reviewer-requested, post hoc check of the original GLORIA spectra explains most missing field-band values as limited wavelength coverage. Among the 571 rows first excluded for unavailable field NIR, the last finite field wavelength was below 800 nm in 74 rows, 800–849 nm in 479 and 850–899 nm in 18. Of the 34 satellite-high rows in this excluded group, 32 ended at 800–849 nm and two below 800 nm. These are matchup-row counts, including repeat uses of a field spectrum, and a row may also have another exclusion reason. The source-linked [coverage registry](../data/aquacorr_validation/field_coverage_registry.json) and [calculation](../data/aquacorr_validation/field_coverage_diagnostic.py) retain the details.

The 348 eligible pairs contain 250 distinct GLORIA field IDs and 103 satellite scene IDs in 37 named waterbody clusters. Eight AquaCorr NIR values exceed 0.30 sr⁻¹: five Landsat 8 and three Sentinel-2B rows. They represent seven distinct field IDs and six scene IDs. **None of the eight corresponding convolved field spectra exceeds 0.30 sr⁻¹.** Their median field NIR is 0.000221 sr⁻¹, and their median signed satellite-minus-field difference is 0.593 sr⁻¹. One Maggiore field spectrum (`GID_860`) appears in two overlapping Sentinel-2 tile rows, so those are not independent field observations. A same-day analysis without the three-hour limit has 481 pairs and 11 satellite-high pairs, again with zero field-high pairs. These are selected-sample findings, not a false-positive rate for AquaCorr or a classifier validation.

Across all eligible pairs, median satellite NIR is 0.01439 sr⁻¹, median convolved field NIR 0.000939 sr⁻¹, and median signed difference 0.01256 sr⁻¹. The equal-waterbody mean difference is 0.06405 sr⁻¹; the median of waterbody means is 0.02182 sr⁻¹. The conditional percentile bootstrap interval for the mean is 0.02684 to 0.11719 sr⁻¹. There is no p-value and the interval must not be read as an externally valid precision claim. The full per-waterbody table is [`waterbody_bias.csv`](../data/aquacorr_validation/waterbody_bias.csv).

The initial fresh-context reviewer noted the influence of a few clusters and missing upstream flow. In the resulting **post hoc** diagnostics, waterbody means range from −0.00353 to 0.82329 sr⁻¹; 36 of 37 are positive. The largest mean is based on only two English Channel pairs. Removing any one waterbody changes the overall equal-waterbody mean to between 0.04296 and 0.06593 sr⁻¹. Removing the eight satellite-high pairs lowers the descriptive mean of the remaining 35 waterbodies to 0.02607 sr⁻¹. These checks show that the extreme tail contributes substantially to the mean, while a smaller positive difference remains in the selected remainder. They do not resolve spatial dependence or selection. All values and the first-reason sensor composition are in [`additional_diagnostics_registry.json`](../data/aquacorr_validation/additional_diagnostics_registry.json).

The Sentinel-2 v5-versus-v4 field-bandpass difference had median −2.26×10⁻⁸ sr⁻¹ among eligible Sentinel pairs, far smaller than the observed discrepancies. That sensitivity result does not address spatial or temporal mismatch.

## Same-acquisition scene checks

For all eight field-paired high-NIR cases, the [scene audit registry](../data/aquacorr_validation/scene_qa_registry.json) records original scene/tile IDs, field points, catalogue responses, point-query URLs, and raw QA values. The five Landsat 8 high rows fall in three acquisitions; each point has USGS `QA_PIXEL` cloud bit set and water bit unset. Two different-date controls from the same broad waterbody labels have water bit set and cloud bit unset; the English Channel control is geographically distant from its high-NIR points and is not a local spatial control. For Maggiore, the two overlapping high Sentinel-2 tile rows have `SCL=4` (vegetation), whereas lower-NIR points in the same two tiles have `SCL=6` (water). The Hou Lake high row's field coordinate lies outside the claimed tile's published bounding box, so its tile QA could not be sampled as claimed. These masks classify **one field-coordinate pixel**; AquaCorr NIR is an ACOLITE-derived 3×3 window statistic. The queried Sentinel-2 SCL asset is 20 m. These observations support scene or matchup concerns **for those cases**; they do not establish whether the cause is a wrong pixel, an archive join, a shoreline footprint, or atmospheric correction.

The [pilot protocol](../data/aquacorr_validation/scene_pilot_protocol.json) then selected five high-NIR (>0.30 sr⁻¹) and five low-NIR (0–0.03 sr⁻¹) same-scene, same-source pairs per sensor, excluding GLORIA and centroid coordinates. Exact selected IDs, coordinates, deterministic SHA-256 selection priority, and source hash are in [`scene_pilot_selection.json`](../data/aquacorr_validation/scene_pilot_selection.json). This is an extreme-tail convenience pilot, not a random validation sample or a local spatial control: the median great-circle separation of high and low points is 64.49 km, and 28 of 30 pairs are more than 10 km apart, as recorded in the post hoc [distance registry](../data/aquacorr_validation/scene_pilot_distance_registry.json).

Among 20 high-NIR Landsat 5/7/8/9 pilot points, 15 have the QA cloud bit set. Among 20 low-NIR Landsat controls, 16 have the QA water bit set. Other bit combinations, including cloud shadow and dilated cloud, are preserved in [`scene_pilot_qa_registry.json`](../data/aquacorr_validation/scene_pilot_qa_registry.json). One early Landsat 9 pair had same-acquisition L2 QA but no matching Level-1 item returned by the USGS STAC query; the gap is disclosed, not silently filled. [USGS states](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-quality-assessment-bands) that Level-1 `QA_PIXEL` is carried unchanged into Level-2; we sampled the public L2 copy. For Landsat 4–7, the [known clear-bit issue](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-known-issues) is why the clear bit was not used as decisive evidence.

Of ten high-NIR Sentinel-2 pilot points, five have `SCL=9` (high-probability cloud), one has `SCL=8` (medium-probability cloud), and four could not be sampled in the claimed tile because their points lie outside that tile's published bounding box. All ten low-NIR controls have `SCL=6` (water). For three scenes with two public L2A product versions, both versions were sampled and agreed on the SCL class. The [Copernicus product specification](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/d/sentinel/s2-pdgs-cs-di-psd-v15-0) defines these class codes. SCL is an algorithmic label; it is not a human adjudication of water truth.

## What remains open

1. **Generalisation and an untouched holdout.** GLORIA is already part of AquaCorr's published validation material. Most `source=GLORIA` rows were screened out, especially for missing field NIR, and the remaining waters are unevenly represented. The scene pilot samples deliberately extreme values. Neither analysis estimates a population error rate, threshold sensitivity/specificity, or performance on an independent region or dataset.
2. **Native pixel and processing mechanism.** We did not inspect georeferenced Level-1 image cutouts or the AquaCorr 3×3 extraction windows, manually adjudicate the water pixels, rerun ACOLITE, or reconcile every scene/tile anomaly. QA associations suggest problems in selected cases but cannot assign a single cause.
3. **Spectral and downstream scope.** GLORIA ends at 900 nm, so no SWIR purity check is possible from these field spectra. We did not test chlorophyll retrieval, model transfer, or the effect of removing high-NIR cases on a downstream task.
4. **Scientific and editorial review.** The bounded literature check did not establish global novelty. The first ARP manuscript was blocked at independent review with major concerns about method precision, exclusions, and waterbody influence. This report supplies the requested diagnostics. A second fresh-context AI reviewer found no major concern and judged the report ready for human review, while explicitly leaving source-download verification, the bootstrap rerun, protocol chronology, native imagery, and author declarations outside that review. The author has not granted final approval. A journal may still reject the work.
